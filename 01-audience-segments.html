Version 2 replaces version 1 and withdraws its headline

Version 1 said the most efficient segment might be the least qualified. Both halves of that were wrong.

What v1 said: 8 of 17 leads came from companies under 50 employees, below the $7,500 monthly floor, so the cheapest leads might be the most expensive waste. That rested on DemandSense company intelligence, which resolves only 67% of clicks to a company and infers lead attribution from that partial match.

What Campaign Manager says: all 5 attributable conversions sit in the 51 to 200 employee band. Every band under 50 employees shows zero. Enterprise share of clicks is 31.6%, not the 53% v1 reported. LinkedIn first-party demographics has full coverage and supersedes the inferred version.

And the real answer on lead quality is worse, for a different reason. Cross-referencing the Pipedrive export against ad exposure shows 10 of the 17 LinkedIn attributed leads have no company name and no business email address. They are individuals who downloaded a gated report with a personal address. The qualification problem is real. It is not a company size problem.

Window and cost caveats

This report covers 1 May to 28 July. The room's spine is 1 March to 28 July.

The Campaign Manager demographics export only runs from 1 May. All 17 leads and all recorded conversions fall inside that window, so conversion analysis is unaffected, but impression and click totals here are not comparable to the 230,251 and 6,421 quoted elsewhere in the room. The base here is 148,199 impressions.

There is still no cost column. LinkedIn does not expose spend by demographic segment. Where dollars appear below they are apportioned by impression share and labelled as estimates. True cost per lead by segment remains unavailable.

Percentages do not sum to 100. LinkedIn assigns members to multiple industry and function segments, so shares overlap by design. Read them as relative weight, not as a partition.

The ICP band is the converting band

All 5 conversions sit in 51 to 200 employees, at a 0.98% conversion rate. The 201 to 500 band engages at 1.87% CTR with no conversions yet, which makes it the offer-gap target rather than a targeting problem. Together those two bands take 28% of impressions.

Still true

Enterprise still buys nothing

10,001+ takes 22.7% of impressions and 31.6% of clicks at the highest CTR of any large band, 2.86%, and converts zero times. The magnitude is smaller than v1 claimed but the direction holds. This is the segment the enterprise case study creative attracts, per Report 4.

Corrected

Sub-50 is not the lead source

1 employee, 2 to 10 and 11 to 50 together take 21.6% of clicks and produce zero conversions. v1 attributed 8 leads here. That attribution came from partial company resolution and does not survive first-party data.

The question this raises

Business Development plus Sales take 56.7% of all impressions. Marketing takes 19.8% and has the best click through rate of the three. For an agency selling marketplace growth services to consumer brands, the buyer sits in marketing, ecommerce or operations. A targeting mix weighted to BD and Sales is consistent with reaching agency and vendor sellers rather than brand side buyers, and it lines up with Advertising Services taking 17.3% of impressions. Worth testing a marketing and operations weighted variant against the current mix.

The thread that connects three datasets

A third of the demand this account attracts is individuals, not brands.

Ten of seventeen LinkedIn leads have no company. 74.5% of Google spend went to affiliate and influencer keywords, which are searched by people wanting to become an Amazon affiliate rather than brands hiring an agency. 6,887 page views sit on the careers page and a 2022 resume helper file.

Three independent datasets, one failure mode. It is not a channel problem and it is not a creative problem. The offer and the keywords are attracting career and side income intent alongside commercial intent, and the measurement layer cannot tell them apart. Fixing that is worth more than any budget reallocation in this room.

07

Recommendations

1 / lead with this

Report the 70.3% decision maker share

Director and above takes 104,129 of 148,199 impressions. This is the strongest defensible targeting result in the account and it is currently missing from every client facing artifact.

2 / reallocate inside targeting

Weight toward Marketing and Operations, away from BD and Sales

BD and Sales take 56.7% of impressions. Marketing takes 19.8% at the best CTR. Marketing Director, CMO, Brand Specialist and Digital Specialist are the four best title profiles and take 4.3% combined.

3 / fix the offer, not the audience

Target the 201 to 500 band with a bottom funnel offer

1.87% CTR, 319 clicks, zero conversions, and squarely able to pay the minimum. The band engages and then stops. Same conclusion as v1, now on better evidence.

4 / stop buying the wrong intent

Gate the report downloads

Ten of seventeen leads arrived with a personal email and no company. Require a business email on the lead form, or accept that the lead count is inflated and report a qualified subset instead.

5 / exclusions

Suppress Advertising Services and IT Services at the industry level

Advertising Services alone absorbed 25,695 impressions, 17.3% of delivery. Combined with Technology and Internet and IT Services, non buyer categories dominate the mix.

6 / verify before presenting

Cross check the 5 conversions against the named leads

All 5 are tagged Business Consulting and Services. That is a broad category with overlapping tags, but it is not the consumer brand label we would want. Confirm who they are before this becomes a slide.
