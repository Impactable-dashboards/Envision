The one paragraph read

A working engagement engine attached to a broken measurement layer, and a creative strategy that is manufacturing its own audience problem.

Across 149 days and $24,411, LinkedIn built genuine reach into the right category and, since June, into the right company sizes. It has produced 17 attributed leads, of which 6 resolve to a real brand. Conversion counting is unsettled: Campaign Manager records 5 for 1 May to 28 July, DemandSense records 0 at campaign grain, Google records 2. That reconciliation is still open. What is certain is that no revenue or closed won data exists, because the CRM export arrived without outcome fields.

Three findings sit underneath that, and they connect. 482 accounts at very high engagement produced 11 of 17 leads on 3.9% of impressions, so the ABM thesis works. 240 accounts absorbed 34.5% of all delivery for one lead, so the distribution is wrong. And the highest engaging creative in the account, the enterprise beauty case studies, has produced nothing from $3,518, while attracting exactly the enterprise segment that converts worst.

The through line: three independent datasets say a large share of the demand this account attracts is individuals, not brands. Ten of the seventeen LinkedIn attributed leads arrived with no company and a personal email. 74.5% of Google spend went to affiliate and influencer keywords, searched by people wanting to become an Amazon affiliate. 6,887 page views sit on the careers page and a 2022 resume helper file. One failure mode, three channels, and the measurement layer cannot tell commercial intent from career intent.

Almost nothing here needs more budget. It needs that distinction made, the warm pools rebalanced, and the creative pointed at mid market brands instead of enterprise logos.

02

The five findings that matter

The proof

482 accounts produced 65% of all leads on 3.9% of impressions

The very high engagement tier converts at 2.89%, roughly 7.4 times the account average. The ABM thesis is not theoretical here, it is measurable. Report 2.

The waste

240 accounts took a third of all delivery for one lead

314 impressions per account against 18 at the top tier, an estimated $8,427. Three campaigns are hitting the same 3,500 person pool 28 times each while a pool of 13,500 gets 1.2. Reports 2 and 3.

The creative trap

The best performing creative is the worst converting creative

Enterprise beauty case studies: 6.01% CTR, $2.00 CPC, zero leads from $3,518. The seasonal tactical playbook: 1.37% CTR, five times the CPC, $74.15 CPL and every recorded conversion. Report 4.

The qualification answer

Ten of seventeen leads have no company at all

Not a company size problem. Ten arrived with a personal email and no company name. Six resolve to real brands: Qunol twice, Clarins, Kids II, Kao, Iceburged. Defensible qualified count is closer to 6. Reports 1 and 2.

The structural gap

Bottom of funnel does not exist, and paid drives 0.29% of site traffic

Six of seven BOF rows are gaps. Paid delivers 44 of 15,353 sessions while organic search delivers 4,093 and AI assistants 389. Report 3.

The search channel

Google beats LinkedIn on every measure available

13.77% CTR against 2.79%. $1.51 CPC against $3.80. And it is the only channel with recorded conversions, two of them. Report 3.

The best defensible result

70.3% of impressions reach Director level and above

Director 34.6%, VP 13.5%, CXO 8.0%, Owner 9.8%, Partner 4.4%, from LinkedIn first party demographics. And all five recorded conversions sit in the 51 to 200 employee band, squarely inside ICP. Report 1.

The turn

June to July, enterprise went from 18 of 30 to zero

Replaced by Dr. Squatch, OLAPLEX, Moroccanoil, Cymbiotika, Thrive Market, Lovevery, BARK. Clicks fell 264 to 101 and leads 2 to 0. Quality up, volume down. Report 2.
